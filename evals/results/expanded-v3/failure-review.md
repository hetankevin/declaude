# Expanded v3 failure inspection

The ten-configuration benchmark keeps the original grader and frozen v3 skill. It contains 320 outputs, including the original 96 responses, all regraded in randomized matched-condition pools. It is a reference for the later revision, not the final calibrated-grader comparison.

Across the eight original held-out cases, all-assertion passes rise from 87.7% to 91.2% and holistic scores from 3.89 to 4.26. The assertion difference is uncertain (+3.49 pp, case-bootstrap interval −1.67 to +8.84). Proof assertions fall from 98.3% to 81.7%. These results must not be replaced by the earlier smaller matrix's stronger estimates.

Luna is the clearest configuration-level regression: assertions fall from 74.4% to 67.4% and holistic from 3.12 to 2.88. Its rewritten editor letter loses which comments were accepted or rejected. Its delivery-semantics explanation reverses a causal relationship and drops an untested-status qualification. Other losses concern overcompression and proof presentation. These are short-text failures; they do not establish a general model ranking.

Some old-grader failures are debatable. It penalizes using “regardless of stability” to express that in-place memory behavior is independent of stability, and treats several mathematically implied statements as missing factual content. Proof assertions also require unusually explicit elementary arithmetic in the original tasks. A missing display step can be a real instruction-following failure without making the derivation mathematically false. The original grader sometimes conflates these. Its verdicts remain unchanged and its exact evidence is published.

The grader revision is evaluated separately on fixed calibration fixtures. The revised skill is selected only from development outcomes and then tested on a fresh set. Do not interpret a difference between old-grader and new-grader scores as a skill improvement.
